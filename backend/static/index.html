    Three questions, in order

    1

    Does this case exist?

    A straight database lookup against millions of real decisions. No AI in this step at all — whether a case exists is a fact, not a judgement, so we never let a model decide it.

    2

    Does it actually say that?

    The harder failure. A real case cited for something it never held looks fine when you look it up. We read the opinion and check whether it supports the claim.

    3

    Is it still good law?

    We follow the citation graph forward, looking for later courts that overruled or undercut it. A warning signal — not a professional citator.

    We check our own work

    An auditing tool that invents its findings is worse than no tool. So before any quote is shown, the software confirms those words appear verbatim in the source opinion. If they don't match, the finding is discarded rather than displayed — the auditor is not allowed to assert what it cannot show.

    What this does not do

    • It does not give legal advice. It checks whether citations are accurate. Applying law to your situation is what lawyers are for.
    • Step 3 is a signal, not a citator. There is no open Shepard's or KeyCite. We scan later opinions for overruling language and say so plainly.
    • Scanned PDFs need OCR, which this does not do. Text-based PDFs are fine.
