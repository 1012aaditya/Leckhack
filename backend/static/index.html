    How it works

    Three questions, asked in order

    00

    Could it exist at all?

    Before any lookup: reporters publish over known date ranges, so 42 F.2d 300 (2015) is impossible — F.2d ended in 1993. No database, no model, no network. It answers even for reporters we hold no case law for.

    01

    Does this case exist?

    A straight database lookup against millions of real decisions. No AI in this step at all — whether a case exists is a fact, not a judgement, so we never let a model decide it.

    02

    Does it actually say that?

    The harder failure. A real case cited for something it never held looks perfectly fine when you look it up. We read the opinion and check whether it supports the claim.

    03

    Is it still good law?

    We follow the citation graph forward, looking for later courts that overruled or undercut it. A warning signal — not a professional citator.

    We check our own work

    An auditing tool that invents its findings is worse than no tool. So before any quote is shown, the software confirms those words appear verbatim in the source opinion. If they don't match, the finding is discarded rather than displayed — the auditor is not allowed to assert what it cannot show.

    Honest limits

    What this does not do

    • It does not give legal advice

      It checks whether citations are accurate. Applying law to your situation is what lawyers are for.

    • Step 3 is a signal, not a citator

      There is no open Shepard's or KeyCite. We scan later opinions for overruling language and say so plainly.

    • A partial corpus cannot prove absence

      We track which reporter volumes we hold completely. Outside them, a missing case is reported as unchecked — never as fabricated.
